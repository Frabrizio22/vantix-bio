## Why Peptides Are Always Out of Stock

### The Bulk Import Trap

Most research peptide vendors follow the same playbook:

1. **Order 500-1,000 vials** from a Chinese manufacturer (4-8 week lead time)
2. **Wait for import** (customs, shipping, delays)
3. **Send samples for testing** (2-3 weeks for third-party COA)
4. **List for sale** once the batch clears

**Total time to restock:** 8-12 weeks minimum.

**Why order in bulk?** Because per-unit manufacturing cost drops at scale:

- 100 vials: $12-15 per vial
- 500 vials: $6-8 per vial
- 1,000+ vials: $3-5 per vial

**The catch:** You need to sell through 500-1,000 vials before ordering again. If you're a small vendor doing $10-20k/month, that's 2-4 months of inventory for ONE peptide.

**What happens when you run out early?**

- You can't "rush order" 50 vials — manufacturers have minimums
- You can't swap suppliers mid-cycle — each batch needs new testing
- You're stuck waiting 8-12 weeks while customers go elsewhere

### The Calendar Problem

Even if you plan perfectly, external factors break the cycle:

**Chinese New Year (Jan-Feb):** Manufacturing shuts down for 2-3 weeks. If you didn't order early enough, your restock pushes into March.

**Golden Week (October):** Similar 1-week delay in production and shipping.

**Port backlogs:** Shipping delays can add 1-2 weeks unpredictably.

**Regulatory changes:** China's peptide export rules changed in March 2024, creating sudden supply disruptions for individual buyers.

**Result:** Even well-planned vendors end up with 4-6 week stockouts multiple times per year.

## The Small-Batch Alternative

We chose a different model that prioritizes **availability over margin**.

### How It Works

Instead of ordering 500-1,000 vials from overseas:

**We order 50-100 vials per SKU from US manufacturers every 3-4 weeks.**

**Manufacturing:** US-based CGMP facilities with 7-14 day turnaround  
**Testing:** Janoshik verification (7-10 business days)  
**Restocking cycle:** 3-4 weeks total

**Economics:**

- **Higher per-unit cost:** $8-10/vial vs. $3-5/vial overseas bulk
- **Lower capital requirements:** $800-1,000 per SKU vs. $3,000-5,000
- **Faster response:** If a peptide sells out in week 2, we reorder immediately (not waiting months)

### Why This Prevents Stockouts

**1. Smaller inventory batches = more frequent restocks**

If we order 50 vials and sell 15/week, we're reordering in week 3-4 (not week 10-12).

**2. Predictable domestic lead times**

US manufacturers don't shut down for Chinese New Year. Shipping is 2-3 days, not weeks. No customs delays.

**3. Flexible minimums**

We can order 25 vials if needed for a rush restock. Chinese manufacturers won't accept orders under 100-500 vials.

**4. Lower financial risk**

If a peptide stops selling (regulatory changes, market shift), we're not stuck with 500 unsold vials. We adjust quickly.

## The Vendor Reliability Test

**Before ordering from any peptide vendor, check these:**

### 1. How many SKUs are actually in stock right now?

If 8 out of 12 products show "out of stock," their supply chain is broken.

**Red flag:** Vendor launched with 15 peptides, only 3-4 ever show available.

**Good sign:** 70%+ of catalog consistently in stock, with visible restock cycles.

### 2. Do they give real restock dates?

"Notify me" with no timeline = they have no idea when it's coming.

**Test:** Sign up for a waitlist. If you don't get an update in 3 weeks, they're not managing inventory seriously.

### 3. How often do "temporarily unavailable" peptides come back?

Some vendors list peptides they've never actually stocked (vaporware catalog to capture search traffic).

**Red flag:** "Coming soon" for 6+ months.

**Good sign:** Product goes out of stock, comes back 3-4 weeks later with a new batch ID.

### 4. Can you see batch IDs before ordering?

If every product just says "In Stock" with no batch number, you don't know if:

- It's actually tested yet
- The COA is from this batch or an old one
- Multiple batches are being mixed together

**Good sign:** Every product page shows current batch (e.g., "VX-TIRZ30-008 - In Stock, 23 vials available").

## Why We Don't Stock Everything

**Full transparency:** We intentionally keep a smaller catalog (12 core peptides) instead of listing 30-50 SKUs.

**Reason:** We'd rather have **consistent availability on 12 peptides** than spotty availability on 30.

**The math:**

- Stocking 12 SKUs with 50-100 vials each = $15-20k inventory
- Stocking 30 SKUs with the same depth = $40-50k inventory

At our current revenue ($4-6k/month), we can't tie up $40k in peptide inventory. So we chose:

**Option A:** 30 peptides, most out of stock 60% of the time  
**Option B:** 12 peptides, in stock 85%+ of the time

**We picked B.**

When we hit $25-30k/month revenue, we'll expand to 20-25 SKUs. But we won't add a peptide unless we can keep it in stock.

## The Supply Chain Lesson

**The stockout problem isn't about lazy vendors.** It's about economic incentives:

- Bulk ordering maximizes margin but creates long stockout windows
- Domestic small-batch costs more but keeps inventory flowing
- Most vendors optimize for profit per vial (not availability)

**We optimized for availability** because researchers don't care about your margin — they care about whether the peptide is actually there when they need it.

**Trade-off:** We make less per vial, but we don't lose sales to stockouts.

**Bet:** Reliable availability builds more long-term customers than rock-bottom pricing with constant "out of stock" frustration.

## Related Questions

### "Why not just order bigger batches from US manufacturers?"

We could, but:

1. **Capital lockup:** $1,000/SKU every 3 weeks is manageable. $5,000/SKU every 12 weeks would drain cash flow.
2. **Overstocking risk:** If demand drops mid-cycle, we're stuck with unsold vials. Smaller batches = lower waste.
3. **Flexibility:** We can adjust order size based on demand trends (50 vials if slow, 100 if popular).

### "What if your US manufacturer goes out of business?"

We work with **two manufacturers** so we're not single-sourced. If one has delays, we can shift orders.

Also: US manufacturers are easier to replace than Chinese suppliers. Domestic lead time for onboarding a new partner is 2-3 weeks (vs. 8-12 weeks overseas).

### "Can you guarantee a peptide will never go out of stock?"

**No.** Honest answer:

- If demand spikes 3x unexpectedly, we might sell out before the next batch arrives
- If Janoshik testing is delayed 2+ weeks, restocks push
- If a manufacturer has equipment failure, we scramble for alternatives

**But:** Our average in-stock rate is 85-90% across all SKUs. Compare to competitors averaging 40-60%.
