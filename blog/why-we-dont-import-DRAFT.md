## The Default Playbook

If you're starting a research peptide business in 2026, here's the standard advice:

1. **Find a Chinese manufacturer** (Welon, Yimei, Shanghai Baoju, Homopeptides)
2. **Order 500-1,000 vials per SKU** (bulk discount pricing)
3. **Send samples to Janoshik** for third-party verification

**Why this works:**

**We looked at this model. We chose not to use it.**

## Why We Don't Import from China

### Reason 1: Lead Times Are Too Long

**Bulk import timeline:**

1. Order from manufacturer: **1-2 days**
2. Wait for synthesis + fulfillment: **4-8 weeks**
3. Customs + international shipping: **1-2 weeks**
4. Janoshik testing after arrival: **2-3 weeks**

**Total:** 8-13 weeks from order to shelf.

**What this means for researchers:**

If a peptide sells out in week 6, you're waiting **another 8+ weeks** for the next batch.

If Chinese New Year hits (Jan-Feb, 2-3 week shutdown), add another month.

If customs has delays, add 1-2 weeks.

**Result:** Stockouts lasting 10-16 weeks are normal in the industry.

**We wanted faster restocks.** So we needed shorter lead times.

---

### Reason 2: Capital Gets Locked Up for Months

**Bulk import economics:**

Let's say you want to stock **BPC-157 10mg**.

- Order 500 vials × $5/vial = **$2,500 upfront**
- Testing: **$305 (Janoshik)**
- Shipping/import: **$200**
- **Total:** **$3,000+ per SKU**

**If you want to stock 10 peptides?** $30,000 tied up in inventory.

**For a small vendor doing $5-10k/month revenue,** that's 3-6 months of gross revenue locked in peptide inventory that won't sell for months.

**What happens if:**

- Demand drops mid-cycle (regulatory changes, competitor pricing)?
- A new peptide becomes popular and you need capital to stock it?
- You miscalculate demand and ordered too much?

**You're stuck with unsold inventory** and no cash to respond.

**We wanted financial flexibility.** So we needed lower inventory commitments.

---

### Reason 3: You Can't Respond to Demand Changes

**Problem:** You ordered 500 vials of Tesamorelin 3 months ago. It's selling slower than expected. You have 380 vials left.

Meanwhile, Tirzepatide is selling 2x faster than projected. You're down to 15 vials and orders are accelerating.

**What can you do?**

❌ **You can't "rush order" 50 more Tirz vials.** Chinese manufacturers have 100-500 vial minimums.

❌ **You can't cancel the slow-moving Tesa order.** It's already made and shipped.

❌ **You can't shift capital** from one SKU to another mid-cycle.

**Result:** You run out of the hot-selling peptide for 8+ weeks while sitting on 380 vials of slow inventory.

**We wanted demand flexibility.** So we needed lower order minimums.

---

### Reason 4: Import Risk Is Real

**Things that can (and do) go wrong:**

**Chinese New Year (Jan-Feb):** Factories shut down 2-3 weeks. If you didn't order early enough, your restock pushes into March.

**Golden Week (October):** 1-week production halt + shipping backlog.

**Port congestion:** Los Angeles/Long Beach ports can add 1-2 weeks to transit unexpectedly.

**Customs delays:** Peptides sometimes get flagged for additional inspection (adds days to weeks).

**Regulatory changes:** In March 2024, China updated peptide export rules, creating sudden supply disruptions for individual buyers.

**Vendor shutdown risk:** In March 2026, Peptide Sciences (one of the largest US vendors) abruptly shut down operations due to regulatory pressure. Researchers relying on them scrambled to find alternatives.

**One of these happens every few months.** Over a 12-month cycle, you'll face multiple 2-4 week delays that compound.

**We wanted predictable timelines.** So we needed domestic sourcing.

3. **No economy of scale** - 50-vial batches cost more per unit than 1,000-vial batches
4. **Raw material sourcing** - US suppliers charge more than bulk Chinese amino acid suppliers

**So yes, our COGS is 60-100% higher per vial.**

## What We Lose by Not Importing

**Let's be fair:** The bulk import model has real advantages we sacrifice.

### Lower per-unit cost

**China bulk:** $3-5/vial  
**US small-batch:** $8-10/vial

**Impact:** We could theoretically charge $35-45/vial if we imported. We charge $55-70 instead.

**Trade-off:** Cheaper peptides with longer stockouts vs. slightly pricier peptides always in stock.

---

### Economy of scale

**China:** Once you're ordering 1,000+ vials, per-unit costs drop further ($2-3/vial possible at 5,000-vial scale).

**US:** 50-100 vials is our typical batch. Ordering 500 vials doesn't reduce cost much ($8 → $7/vial at best).

**Impact:** We can't compete on rock-bottom pricing with mega-vendors doing $500k+/year revenue.

**Trade-off:** We stay small and flexible vs. scaling to ultra-low-cost high-volume.

---

### Bulk bargaining power

**China:** Large orders = leverage to negotiate better terms, priority production slots, custom synthesis.

**US:** 50-100 vials = we're a small customer. Less negotiating power.

**Impact:** We pay market rates for US manufacturing. Bulk importers get volume discounts.

**Trade-off:** Fair pricing at small scale vs. aggressive pricing at massive scale.

## How to Evaluate Any Peptide Vendor's Supply Chain

**Before you order, ask:**

### 1. Where do they manufacture?

**Red flag:** Won't say, vague "trusted partners" language  
**Good sign:** Specific region (US, EU, China), transparent about trade-offs

### 2. How often do they restock?

**Red flag:** Products out of stock for 8+ weeks with no ETA  
**Good sign:** Visible restock cycles, real dates (not "notify me")

### 3. What's their in-stock rate?

**Test:** Check 10 random products. How many show "in stock" vs. "out of stock"?

**Red flag:** <50% in stock  
**Good sign:** 70-90% in stock consistently

### 4. Do they show batch IDs?

**Red flag:** Generic "In Stock" with no batch number  
**Good sign:** Every product shows current batch (e.g., VX-TIRZ30-009)

### 5. Can you verify COAs independently?

**Red flag:** PDF with no external verification  
**Good sign:** Janoshik or Finn rick task ID you can check yourself
